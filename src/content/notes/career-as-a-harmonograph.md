The drawing beside the timeline on the About page is a harmonograph: a pen moved by a chain of
spinning circles, one for each role or degree. Scroll from the bottom of the list to the top and a
circle joins the chain for every entry you pass, until the whole flower appears.

## Why five petals

The two newest entries spin at frequencies 2 and −3, in opposite directions. Two circles whose
speeds differ by 5 trace a figure with exactly five petals, the same rose as the site mark. Every
older entry gets a frequency with the same remainder when divided by 5 (7, −8, 12, −13), which
keeps the five-fold symmetry as circles are added: older roles embroider the flower rather than
changing its shape.

## Sizes that grow

Circle sizes increase with each entry, from 0.15 for the first degree to 1 for the current role.
Each new circle is the largest so far, so it sets the outline and everything before it becomes
texture. That is why each step looks different: a dot, a toothed gear, a star, a swirl and finally
the flower.
